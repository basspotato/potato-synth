# Roland JP-8080 (1998): clean virtual-analog oscillators as a reference

Scope: what is specific to the JP-8000/JP-8080 and its oscillators. General supersaw maths (detune ratios, key-tracked HPF) is in the sibling notes file and is not repeated here, except where new primary data (the 2025 DSP reverse-engineering) refines it.

## Oscillator architecture (OSC1/OSC2 waveforms, controls, sync/ring/X-mod, pitch env, balance)

### Takeaway
There are two oscillators per voice. OSC1 has seven waves (Super Saw, Triangle Mod, Noise, Feedback Osc, Square/PWM, Saw, Triangle), and each wave gets two wave-specific sliders. OSC2 is a simpler Square(PWM)/Saw/Triangle oscillator, plus Noise on the 8080, and it adds sync, ring and cross-mod. The "huge" Super Saw is a single OSC1 waveform, so it costs no extra polyphony.

### Cited Findings
- OSC1 waves on the JP-8000: Super Saw, Triangle Mod, Noise, Feedback, Square, Saw, Triangle. OSC2: Square, Saw, Triangle — [Wikipedia: Roland JP-8000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roland_JP-8000)
- Roland's JP-8080 product page lists 7 waves: "Super Saw, Triangle Modulation, Noise, Feedback Oscillator, Square (PWM), Sawtooth and Triangle" — [Roland JP-8080 product page](https://www.roland.com/global/products/jp-8080/)
- The two OSC1 sliders change function with the wave chosen. These control names come from Roland's JP-8000 brochure/PDF — [Roland JP-8000 PDF](https://static.roland.com/assets/media/pdf/JP-8000.pdf)
  - **Super Saw:** "stacks seven sawtooth waves". DETUNE sets spaciousness and MIX sets body and thickness.
  - **Triangle Mod:** a "triangle rich in overtones". OFFSET changes the shape to add more overtones.
  - **Noise:** continuously variable from pink to white.
  - **Feedback Osc:** feedback amount and harmonic level are user-definable.
  - **Square:** pulse width and PWM depth.
  - **Saw:** a SHAPE parameter "tailors the character".
  - **Triangle:** few overtones and fairly neutral, suited to flute-like sounds.
- SOS describes Triangle Mod as a wave-shape offset that "bends and twists" the triangle, essentially a waveform "wrap" facility. It also says the Feedback wave is monophonic in character — [Sound On Sound JP-8080 review, Nov 1998](https://www.soundonsound.com/reviews/roland-jp8080)
- On the 8080, OSC1 and OSC2 both offer saw, square, triangle and noise. Noise on OSC2 is new on the 8080. Super Saw and Feedback stay OSC1-only — [SOS](https://www.soundonsound.com/reviews/roland-jp8080); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roland_JP-8000)
- OSC2 pitch uses RANGE plus FINE/WIDE, within "+/-50 cent and/or +/-4 octave". SYNC resets OSC2 each time OSC1 returns to its origin. RING gives inharmonic, bell-like tones. CROSS MOD uses OSC2's output to modulate OSC1's frequency, with depth set in OSC Common. OSC Common BALANCE sets the OSC1/OSC2 level — [Roland JP-8000 PDF](https://static.roland.com/assets/media/pdf/JP-8000.pdf)
- The pitch envelope is a simple attack/decay. LFO1 has tri/saw/square/random waves. LFO2 is triangle only and is meant for external controllers. Both can target pitch, filter and amp, with + or − amounts — [SOS](https://www.soundonsound.com/reviews/roland-jp8080)
- Arturia's JUP-8000 V keeps the same split: OSC1 has 7 modes, and OSC2 has Pulse/Saw/Tri with Sync, Wide tuning, PWM, Ring and X-Mod — [Arturia JUP-8000 V](https://www.arturia.com/de/products/software-instruments/jup-8000-v/overview)
- Feedback Osc as described by third parties: "basically just a saw with a keytracked comb filter that self-resonates". In that emulation, Feedback brings in the comb and Harmonics shifts the comb across an octave. At maximum feedback with Harmonics swept, it sounds like "a guitar in front of a very loud amp". This is a forum user's approximation, not Roland documentation — [Vital forum](https://forum.vital.audio/t/roland-style-feedback-oscillator/12153)
- Vintagesynth lists "triangle (PWM)" among the waves — [vintagesynth.com](https://www.vintagesynth.com/roland/jp-8080). This conflicts with Roland's description of Triangle Mod as an OFFSET/shape control, and Roland's description is preferred.

### Inferences
- Treat each OSC1 waveform as one "algorithm with 2 macro knobs" (CTRL1/CTRL2), which maps cleanly onto a modern plugin oscillator design:
  - Super Saw: Detune/Mix.
  - Square: PW/PWM depth.
  - Triangle Mod: Offset/shape (wavefold/wrap-like).
  - Feedback: Feedback/Harmonic.
  - Saw: Shape.
  - Noise: Colour.
- Feedback Osc probably works like a short delay-line/comb resonator around a saw, with the delay tuned to a harmonic ratio of the note. A Karplus-like or comb-feedback design is a reasonable model, but it is unconfirmed (see Gaps).

### Gaps
- Exact numeric ranges for CTRL1/CTRL2 per wave (all 0–127 on the panel, but the internal mapping is unknown), the OSC2 CTRL1/CTRL2 functions on the 8080, and the X-mod depth range. The full 8080 owner's manual was not retrieved; it sits behind manualslib/Roland support pages.
- No primary source on Feedback Osc or Triangle Mod internals. The 2025 reverse-engineering so far covers only the Super Saw.

## Supersaw on JP-8080 vs JP-8000: Szabo analysis, the 2025 DSP reverse-engineering, and model differences

### Takeaway
Szabo (2010) measured both units and modelled the supersaw as seven naive saws with these traits:
- random phase on each note-on
- a non-linear detune curve (11th-order polynomial)
- a linear-down centre / parabolic-up sides mix
- a pitch-tracked HPF

The 2025 39C3 reverse-engineering of the actual DSP code confirms the structure and shows the implementation is simpler than Szabo's curves suggest:
- 24-bit phase accumulators running at 88.2 kHz
- a fixed integer detune table
- fixed centre gain and linear side gain

The 8080 sounds the same as the 8000.

### Cited Findings
- Szabo's thesis used recordings of both the JP-8000 and JP-8080 ("only these two models were used"), made at 44.1 kHz/32-bit and analysed with a 131072-point FFT — [Szabo 2010 thesis PDF](https://www.adamszabo.com/internet/adam_szabo_how_to_emulate_the_super_saw.pdf)
- Szabo's detune curve, with x from 0 to 1, is: y = 10028.7312891634x^11 − 50818.8652045924x^10 + 111363.4808729368x^9 − 138150.6761080548x^8 + 106649.6679158292x^7 − 53046.9642751875x^6 + 17019.9518580080x^5 − 3425.0836591318x^4 + 404.2703938388x^3 − 24.1878824391x^2 + 0.6717417634x + 0.0030115596. It rises gently up to about 0.5, gets steeper, then rises sharply above 0.9 — [Szabo](https://www.adamszabo.com/internet/adam_szabo_how_to_emulate_the_super_saw.pdf)
- Szabo's mix curves are: centre y = −0.55366x + 0.99785, and sides y = −0.73764x² + 1.2841x + 0.044372. Centre and sides are equal at x = 0.75 (MIDI 95), not at maximum — [Szabo](https://www.adamszabo.com/internet/adam_szabo_how_to_emulate_the_super_saw.pdf)
- Szabo on phase and the HPF:
  - The oscillators are free-running, and each gets a new random phase on every note trigger.
  - Phase must stay static during the note; LFO-modulating it causes artifacts.
  - A pitch-tracked HPF sits after the oscillators, with its cutoff following the fundamental. No order or cutoff value is given.
  - Aliasing "adds airiness" and should be kept, with the HPF removing the harsh aliasing below the fundamental.
  - [Szabo](https://www.adamszabo.com/internet/adam_szabo_how_to_emulate_the_super_saw.pdf)
- 2025 reverse-engineering: Giulioz, "From Silicon to Darude Sand-storm: breaking famous synthesizer DSPs", 39C3, Dec 2025 — [media.ccc.de](https://media.ccc.de/v/39c3-from-silicon-to-darude-sand-storm-breaking-famous-synthesizer-dsps/audio). Findings as summarised by the ÜBERSAW project — [ÜBERSAW algorithm.md](https://github.com/house-of-houses/ubersaw/blob/master/docs/algorithm.md):
  - **Chips and rate:** four custom "ESP2" DSPs (TC170C140) in the JP-8000, running at **88,200 Hz** (67.7376 MHz / (16×3×16)).
  - **Oscillators:** **24-bit integer phase accumulators** that wrap at ±8,388,607 to form the saw, with no wavetable.
  - **Detune table** for the 7 oscillators: **0, +128, −128, +816, −824, +1408, −1440** (asymmetric). Per-oscillator detune = (table[i] × (pitch × detune)) >> 7, so detune scales with pitch.
  - **Mix:** oscillator 0 is always at full level, and oscillators 1–6 are scaled linearly by one "spread"/Mix parameter. Szabo's apparent parabolic and decreasing-centre curves come from the parameter mapping and fixed-point maths, not from explicit curves.
  - **Phase:** random start phases on every note-on.
  - **HPF:** a pitch-tracked HPF after the sum. Its exact topology is undocumented; ÜBERSAW uses a 1-pole, and the doc says "community analysis suggests a multi-pole SVF".
  - These are the project's claims and were not independently verified.
- Synth Anatomy (Jan 2026) on the JE-8086 emulator:
  - The original is "7 naive saw waves… passed through a high-pass filter without modulation, with no phase tricks or chorus".
  - The 88.2 kHz rate is what prevents audible aliasing, and copying the design at lower rates will alias.
  - [Synth Anatomy](https://synthanatomy.com/2026/01/je-8086-roland-dsp-emulator-insights-and-the-supersaw-is-no-longer-a-mystery.html)
- Chip vendor conflict: the ÜBERSAW doc says Toshiba TC170C140, while Synth Anatomy says the TC170C140 (ESP2) was "made by Motorola". Toshiba is more consistent with the TC part prefix.
- The Usual Suspects (Gearmulator, maker of the JE-8086 emulator) give the hardware as a Hitachi H8S MCU plus custom Roland ESP ASICs: **4 in the JP-8000 and 5 in the JP-8080**. The 5th ASIC is what allows 10 voices. These synths do not use the Motorola DSP56300 family — [The Usual Suspects JE-8086](https://theusualsuspects.io/technical/je8086)
- Polyphony and modes:
  - The JP-8000 has 8 voices, and the JP-8080 has 10 voices, or 8 when the Voice Modulator (vocoder) is in use. External audio input costs 2 voices and OSC2 — [SOS](https://www.soundonsound.com/reviews/roland-jp8080)
  - Unison is new on the 8080, and SOS found no sonic difference in A/B tests against the 8000 — [SOS](https://www.soundonsound.com/reviews/roland-jp8080)
  - JP-8000 patches load on the 8080, but not all 8080 patches load on the 8000 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roland_JP-8000)
- The JP-8080 "Voice Modulator" is a 12-band vocoder/formant filter with three modes: Formant Filter, Filter Bank and Vocal Morph Control — [Roland](https://www.roland.com/global/products/jp-8080/). It has a "Robot Voice" oscillator, and Vocal Morph responds mainly around 1.2 kHz — [SOS](https://www.soundonsound.com/reviews/roland-jp8080)

### Inferences
- For modelling, the ÜBERSAW/39C3 data is more authoritative than Szabo's fitted curves. A naive 24-bit phase accumulator at 88.2 kHz, the integer detune table, linear side gain and a key-tracked HPF should reproduce the hardware almost bit-exactly.
- The table offsets relative to the centre (128, 816/824, 1408/1440) correspond to the ratio spread described in the sibling notes at maximum detune.
- The JP-8080 adds no supersaw changes. Its differences are 10 voices (via the 5th ESP), Unison, OSC2 Noise, the vocoder/ext-in, a distortion FX and more memory.

### Gaps
- The brief's "10/20 voices with expansion" is not supported. No source mentions a polyphony expansion for the JP-8080; Roland's page, SOS, vintagesynth and soundprogramming.net all give a fixed 10 voices.
- I could not confirm whether the "voice-modelling processor" the brief mentions is anything other than the Voice Modulator/vocoder.
- The exact HPF order and cutoff ratio relative to the fundamental remain undocumented.

## Why the oscillators are perceived as "clean"

### Takeaway
The cleanness comes from several fixed design traits:
- an unusually high 88.2 kHz internal rate with naive saws, so aliasing mostly sits above audibility
- a pitch-tracked HPF that removes sub-fundamental aliasing and mud
- fixed-point exact tuning with no drift, so the only motion is the static detune beating
- no chorus or phase modulation inside the oscillator

Contemporary reviewers nonetheless heard it as "warm" and analogue-like, but thinner than analogue in unison.

### Cited Findings
- Internal rate 88.2 kHz (Nyquist 44.1 kHz), which "pushes most aliasing above audibility"; naive saws alias below the fundamental and the HPF removes that — [ÜBERSAW algorithm.md](https://github.com/house-of-houses/ubersaw/blob/master/docs/algorithm.md); [Synth Anatomy](https://synthanatomy.com/2026/01/je-8086-roland-dsp-emulator-insights-and-the-supersaw-is-no-longer-a-mystery.html)
- Szabo: aliasing exists and contributes "airiness", and it should be kept rather than removed — [Szabo](https://www.adamszabo.com/internet/adam_szabo_how_to_emulate_the_super_saw.pdf)
- Wikipedia describes the JP-8000 as a "16-bit sound processor" — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roland_JP-8000). This conflicts in spirit with the 24-bit accumulators found by reverse-engineering. The 16-bit figure may refer to the output DACs or data path, which is unclear.
- SOS 1998:
  - Pros list "Realistic, powerful analogue emulation".
  - DSP unison sounds thinner than a real analogue's, "though still pleasing".
  - Aliasing and digital harshness are not discussed.
  - [SOS](https://www.soundonsound.com/reviews/roland-jp8080)
- Perfect Circuit says VAs like the JP-8000 lacked classic analogue warmth, and that the internal HPF that reduces muddiness/aliasing is part of the character — [Perfect Circuit](https://www.perfectcircuit.com/signal/super-saw-history)

### Inferences
- For a "clean oscillator" reference in a new synth, take these properties from the JP-8080:
  - zero pitch drift
  - deterministic integer detune
  - random but static start phase
  - an oversampled naive or lightly band-limited saw, at roughly 2× 44.1 kHz or more
  - a key-tracked HPF
- Extensions such as optional drift, band-limiting and more voices can then be added on top as opt-in features.
- Running naive saws at 44.1/48 kHz will alias more than the hardware did. Oversample to at least 88.2/96 kHz, or use polyBLEP, if aiming for the hardware's perceived cleanliness.

### Gaps
- No measured spectra of high-note aliasing on the hardware were found.
- No source quantified the DAC bit depth or output filtering.

## Filter and how it pairs with the supersaw

### Takeaway
A single resonant multimode filter (LPF/BPF/HPF, 12 or 24 dB/oct, self-oscillating) follows the oscillators. The supersaw's own internal HPF sits before it, inside the oscillator.

### Cited Findings
- LPF/BPF/HPF with a −12/−24 dB switch, FREQUENCY and RESONANCE sliders, and self-oscillation — [Roland JP-8000 PDF](https://static.roland.com/assets/media/pdf/JP-8000.pdf); [SOS](https://www.soundonsound.com/reviews/roland-jp8080)
- Cutoff modulation comes from key-follow, the LFOs and the filter ADSR, and the amp has its own ADSR — [SOS](https://www.soundonsound.com/reviews/roland-jp8080)
- The 8080 also has a 12-band formant filter bank (the Voice Modulator) — [vintagesynth.com](https://www.vintagesynth.com/roland/jp-8080)
- Arturia replicates the filter as LP/BP/HP, 12/24 dB, with velocity-tracked cutoff — [Arturia](https://www.arturia.com/de/products/software-instruments/jup-8000-v/overview)

### Inferences
- The supersaw's HPF is part of the oscillator, separate from the main filter. A new design should keep it inside the oscillator so that the user's filter choice cannot undo the de-mudding.

### Gaps
- No source characterises the filter's sonic character (for example drive or resonance compensation).

## Unison/voice modes, Motion Control, Tone Control, effects

### Takeaway
The trance sound is OSC1 Super Saw, optionally doubled with Unison (8080), through Super Chorus or flanger and panning delay, with the Bass/Treble tone control.

### Cited Findings
- Voice and patch modes are Single, Split, Dual and Unison, plus Mono, Legato and portamento (fixed-time only). Unison stacks all voices with adjustable detune — [SOS](https://www.soundonsound.com/reviews/roland-jp8080)
- Multi-FX chorus types (12): SUPER CHORUS SLW/MID/FST/CLR, FLANGER SLOW/DEEP/FAST, DEEP PHASING SLW, JET PHASING, TWISTING, FREEZE PHASE1/2 — [Roland JP-8000 PDF](https://static.roland.com/assets/media/pdf/JP-8000.pdf)
- Delay types (5): PANNING L→R, PANNING R→L, PANNING SHORT, MONO SHORT, MONO LONG — [Roland JP-8000 PDF](https://static.roland.com/assets/media/pdf/JP-8000.pdf)
- On the 8080:
  - There are 13 modulation FX types, including the new distortion.
  - Delay reaches 1.25 s and offers ping-pong.
  - Modulation FX speed syncs to LFO1 or MIDI clock.
  - Bass/Treble tone control is set per patch.
  - Motion Control records knob moves for up to 99 bars.
  - Memory is 512 patches (128 user) and 256 performances (64 user), with SmartMedia storage.
  - [SOS](https://www.soundonsound.com/reviews/roland-jp8080)
- Other modern trance-style FX ideas, from Arturia's JUP-8000 V extensions: Trance Gate (up to 32 steps), Super Unison, JUN-6 chorus and a multi-band compressor — [Arturia](https://www.arturia.com/de/products/software-instruments/jup-8000-v/overview)

### Gaps
- The DSP topology of the Super Chorus was not found.

## Classic trance/EDM patches and producers

### Takeaway
The best-documented use is Darude's "Sandstorm" (JP-8080), named after a preset pad. Vintagesynth lists BT, Ken Ishii, Faithless and a.vanvranken as users. I found no sourced patch sheets for Ferry Corsten, Armin van Buuren or Kernkraft 400.

### Cited Findings
- Darude's "Sandstorm" prominently features the JP-8080, and the song is named after a pad patch — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roland_JP-8000)
- JP-8080 users: BT, Ken Ishii, Faithless, a.vanvranken — [vintagesynth.com](https://www.vintagesynth.com/roland/jp-8080)
- Recipe guidance:
  - Detune until the individual saws become audible, then back off for punch.
  - Use free-running oscillators.
  - Place an HPF near the fundamental with substantial key-tracking.
  - Add stereo width and chorus/flanger/reverb.
  - [Perfect Circuit](https://www.perfectcircuit.com/signal/super-saw-history)
- Szabo recommends subtle detune for pads, since the curve is flat around the middle of its range — [Szabo](https://www.adamszabo.com/internet/adam_szabo_how_to_emulate_the_super_saw.pdf)

### Gaps
- No verified per-parameter patch values for famous tracks. The KVR and Gearspace patch threads failed to load.
- Ferry Corsten, Armin van Buuren and Kernkraft 400 (Zombie Nation) usage on the JP-8000/8080 is unverified here. Kernkraft 400 is commonly linked to other gear, so do not assert a JP connection without a source.

## Modern recreations

### Takeaway
I found no Roland Cloud "JP-8000 Model Expansion" or ACB product. The main recreations are:
- Arturia JUP-8000 V (circuit-level model plus large extensions)
- the Gearmulator JE-8086 (DSP-level emulation running the original firmware)
- ÜBERSAW (Eurorack, from the reverse-engineered algorithm)
- Szabo's Airwave
- assorted free emulations

Roland itself carries "Super Saw" as an oscillator type in later instruments.

### Cited Findings
- Arturia JUP-8000 V:
  - Claims to "rebuild the circuits of the original JP-8000", with OSC1 offering 7 modes and 16 voices.
  - Adds a 4-slot FX rack (18 types), multi-arp, a 64-step poly sequencer, drag-and-drop modulation, macros, MPE and MTS-ESP.
  - [Arturia](https://www.arturia.com/de/products/software-instruments/jup-8000-v/overview)
- JE-8086 (Gearmulator) emulates the ESP DSPs and MCU rather than re-modelling the synth engine — [The Usual Suspects](https://theusualsuspects.io/technical/je8086); [Synth Anatomy](https://synthanatomy.com/2026/01/je-8086-roland-dsp-emulator-insights-and-the-supersaw-is-no-longer-a-mystery.html)
- Synth Anatomy calls Szabo's Airwave plugin "a very authentic emulation", though "not perfect" — [Synth Anatomy](https://synthanatomy.com/2026/01/je-8086-roland-dsp-emulator-insights-and-the-supersaw-is-no-longer-a-mystery.html)
- ÜBERSAW runs on a Daisy (STM32H750) at 96 kHz, emulating the 24-bit wrap in 32-bit integers — [ÜBERSAW](https://github.com/house-of-houses/ubersaw/blob/master/docs/algorithm.md)
- Supersaw oscillator types also appear in:
  - Roland SH-201, Gaia SH-01, V-Synth/XT and Gaia 2
  - Access Virus TI's "Hypersaw" (2005)
  - SUPERWAVE P8 and Supersaw Plus (2–10 oscillators)
  - [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roland_JP-8000)
- The Roland SH-4d has a Super Saw waveform with detune, "almost certainly modeled off the original" — [Perfect Circuit](https://www.perfectcircuit.com/signal/super-saw-history)
- Roland ACB Boutiques were limited to about 4 voices by processing cost, while the newer ABM/Zen approach gives more polyphony but "may miss some nuances" (SOS, discussing the JX-08) — [SOS JX-08 review](https://www.soundonsound.com/reviews/roland-jx-08)

### Inferences
- The highest-fidelity reference is the JE-8086, which runs the original code, or the ÜBERSAW algorithm.
- Arturia shows the commercial "extend it" direction: more voices, Super Unison, FX rack and modulation matrix.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm a Roland Cloud JP-8000 Model Expansion or a JP-8000 ACB. A search turned up only JD-800, JX-8P and Jupiter-8 expansions.
- Not researched for lack of budget: how the JD-Xi and Jupiter-X supersaw implementations differ (voice count, internal HPF).
